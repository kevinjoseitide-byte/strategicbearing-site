Aircraft Delivery Delays: Stalling Growth, Sustainability, and Efficiency

Ongoing delays in aircraft deliveries are stifling growth, derailing sustainability efforts, and inflating operating costs. And how?

Missed Opportunities for Growth
In 2019, Airbus projected that 64% of new aircraft demand would support growth, with only 36% for replacement. By 2024, those numbers have flipped: growth demand has dwindled to 56%, while airlines scramble to replace aging fleets.

Aging Fleets Take Their Toll
The average global fleet age has hit 14.8 years, the highest since 1990, compared to 13.6 years over the past three decades. With new aircraft in short supply, airlines are left to rely on older, less efficient aircraft.

Fuel Efficiency Stalls
Fuel efficiency (litres/100 ATK terms) remained virtually unchanged in 2024, breaking a 20-year trend of annual improvements between 1.5%-2%. This stagnation is a direct result of older fleets and slower replacement cycles.

Rising Maintenance Costs
Older fleets demand frequent and costly retrofits—Emirates is investing $2 billion in retrofits, partly due to delivery delays. Meanwhile, engine reliability issues in younger fleets exacerbate downtime, leading to a growing number of stored aircraft and further straining operations.

Environmental Impacts
Aging aircraft produce higher CO2 emissions, undercutting the industry's sustainability goals.

Production Delays Persist
Total aircraft deliveries in 2024 stood at 1,114, down 31% from 2018 levels. While Airbus deliveries have nearly recovered (down just 4%), Boeing’s output has plummeted by 57%.

Backlogs Stretch Timelines
Airbus and Boeing have a combined backlog of 14,903 aircraft, which would take over 13 years to clear at current production rates. To meet future demand of 42,430 new aircraft by 2043, production must more than double to 2,400 aircraft annually.

The Ripple Effects for Airlines
•Growth on Hold (Airline launches too)
•Soaring Costs
•Sustainability and Competitiveness Erosion

The confluence of these factors presents a significant challenge to the industry, with solutions remaining elusive.

On the flip side, the forced capacity discipline is aiding yields but that’s not the right/ sustainable way to grow! Is it?
